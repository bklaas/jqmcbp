Aaaaaand We're Back

For the rookies, and those that don't remember what I told you last year:
What I do is send out email updates (personalized just for your entry!) several times during the course of the tournament. I go on and on and on over seemingly meaningless minutia. It might seem meaningless but...well okay, it's meaningless. But we'll have fun, or your money back.**

You will be competing against 849 other humans and 1,001 chimps* for the coveted title of JQMCBP Champion and the accompanying candy bar prize. The top 10 humans and top 10 chimps will be listed in each email update, but you can check the leaderboard at any time to see where you stand. Powered by the Meister's NearRealTimeScoring™ system, the leaderboard is updated within minutes of each game finishing. Or sometimes hours. It really depends on when I click the buttons. Seriously, that's how it works.

In addition to the emails and the leaderboard, there's lots of important, vital resources available with your web browser. Setup a filter with all your friends and enemies to track your own "pool within the pool". Evaluate how risky your picks are with the J Factor, see who picked brackets like yours with the Scientific Similarity Index. As the tournament progresses, figure out who has no chance of winning at the Hall of Shame! Generate pointless head-to-head graphs! Every one of my emails will contain these linky links at the bottom of the email.

As I've done for the past decade plus, I'm sending emails through the appropriately named MailChimp mass email service. This is Lucky the Chimp's favorite email service, and because it actually sends emails that people tend to receive. However, MailChimp will not allow me to send multiple emails to the same email address. So, if you signed up several brackets on one email address, you only be getting one personalized email for each entry. How about we call this a "feature" and call an end to the tech talk.

Last thing: if you like the JQMCBP please remember to smash that Velociraptor! button.

* and 1,001 chimps. This number has been remarkably steady.
